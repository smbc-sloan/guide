Where do I pray, and where do I eat?

Those are the first two practical questions every Muslim arriving at Sloan asks. This guide answers both.

It is written by Muslims at Sloan for Muslims at Sloan. Nothing here is official MIT policy — it is what we have figured out by living here. If something is wrong or out of date, tell the SMBC board so the next version fixes it.

01

MIT MSA — join it alongside SMBC

Where SMBC is your professional and social home inside Sloan, the MIT Muslim Students Association is where you find the rest of Muslim MIT: undergrads, PhD students, postdocs, visiting scholars and staff, plus community members from around Cambridge who pray on campus. It represents several hundred Muslims and runs programming year-round.

Jumu'ah prayer

Every Friday, khutbah 1:15 PM
MIT Musalla, W11-110

Connect

Every Friday, 5:30 PM (in term)
Musalla — free dinner, topics suggested by attendees

Community gatherings

Varies year-round
Dinners, socials, Ramadan iftars, educational programming

How to plug in

1  Subscribe to the mailing list

The single highest-value action for a new student — it is how iftar invitations and event announcements reach you. Sign up from the "Stay Connected" section of msa.mit.edu.

2  Save the prayer times

The MSA publishes live Adhan and Iqama times for the Musalla on MAWAQIT, linked from their prayer page.

3  Show up to Connect

Fridays at 5:30 PM with free dinner. The easiest low-stakes way in.

02

Prayer at MIT

There are two prayer rooms inside the Sloan buildings. Most new students walk twelve minutes to W11 without knowing this.

E52-112

DEDICATED MUSLIM PRAYER ROOM

Sloan Building E52, Room 112

Your default between classes. A purpose-designated Muslim prayer space — not a repurposed meditation room. Already oriented to qibla.

E51-050

MULTI-FAITH PRAYER & MEDITATION

Sloan Building E51, Room 050

Ground floor of E51, across from E52. Non-denominational, so you may be sharing the space with others.

If Dhuhr or Asr is called during a Sloan class, you do not need to cross campus.

Praying in an empty conference room is normal and accepted at Sloan. Book it like any other room.

The Musalla (W11-110)

Daily prayers, Jumu'ah and all MSA programming happen here. It sits in Building W11, the Religious Activity Center, on the west side of campus near the Student Center — roughly a ten to twelve minute walk from E52.

Wudu

A dedicated area inside the building, with separate facilities for brothers and sisters. Bidets are also available in W11.

Access

Entry with your MIT ID. Locked overnight, roughly 1 AM to 4 AM.

Qibla

W11-110 and E52-112 are already oriented. Elsewhere, use a Qibla finder app.

Wudu outside W11 means using standard restrooms. Bring a small towel and clean up — shared-restroom wudu is the most common source of friction with non-Muslim colleagues.

03

Jummah

1:15 PM

KHUTBAH BEGINS

Room W11-110

Finishes by 1:50 – 2:00 PM

Budget the 1:00 to 2:15 block if you are walking back to Sloan afterwards.

Arrive on time

The Musalla fills up — MIT students and community members from around Cambridge both attend. If the main room is full, there is an overflow room in the W11 dining room.

In winter, stay for Asr

Asr comes in early, so attendees are invited to stay a few extra minutes after Jumu'ah to pray it.

Friday 1:15 collides with class and recruiting — plan for it

Friday early afternoon is prime time at Sloan: classes, recruiting events and club programming all land in the 1:00 to 2:30 window. The clash is predictable, which means it is manageable if you handle it at the start of term rather than the week of.

1  Check before you bid

Run your Friday early-afternoon slots against the 1:15 khutbah before you bid on or register for courses.

2  Email the professor early

A short, matter-of-fact note: you observe Friday congregational prayer, the exact window, and what you propose — a recording, notes from a classmate, making up participation. Most Sloan faculty are accommodating when asked early and specifically. Asking the week of is what makes it awkward.

3  Escalate patterns collectively

If several SMBC members hit the same recurring clash, raise it with the program office as a group rather than one by one.

If you cannot make it to W11: Islamic Society of Boston (ISB Cambridge), 204 Prospect St — walkable from Central Square, about 15 minutes from Sloan. Check their current Jummah time before relying on it.

Masjids in Greater Boston

Campus covers daily prayer. Masjids cover Eid, Taraweeh, halaqas, nikah and janazah — and a community that is not all students.

Closest, and the major centres

Islamic Society of Boston (ISB Cambridge)
204 Prospect St, Cambridge, MA 02139

(617) 876-3546 · The closest masjid to campus, and the one with student roots — founded in 1981 out of the Muslim student groups at MIT, Harvard, BU, Northeastern and Tufts. Walkable from Central Square.

Islamic Society of Boston Cultural Center (ISBCC)
100 Malcolm X Blvd, Roxbury, MA 02120

(617) 858-6114 · The largest mosque in New England, with roughly 1,700 at Friday prayers. Your Eid and Taraweeh destination. Orange Line to Roxbury Crossing.

Mosque for the Praising of Allah
724 Shawmut Ave, Roxbury, MA 02119

Boston's oldest masjid — historically significant and rooted in Boston's Black Muslim community.

Yusuf Mosque
186 Chestnut Hill Ave, Brighton, MA 02135

(617) 254-1939 · A community favourite, and convenient if you live in Brighton or Allston.

Islamic Center of New England (ICNE)
470 South St, Quincy, MA 02169 74 Chase Dr, Sharon, MA 02067

Serving Greater Boston since 1964. Quincy is the historic location; Sharon is the larger suburban campus.

05

Halal food

Boston is expensive. Anyone here for two years will cook — so butchers come first.

Where to buy halal meat

Al-Hoda Market CAMBRIDGE
304 Prospect St, Cambridge

Essentially next door to ISB Cambridge — pair a masjid visit with a grocery run.

Foodland CAMBRIDGE
2234 Massachusetts Ave, Cambridge

(617) 349-0009 · Halal meat and international groceries. North Cambridge, Red Line to Davis or Porter.

Hebron Market MISSION HILL
1522 Tremont St, Boston

(617) 238-7896 · Strongest recommendation. Full butcher — beef, lamb, poultry and goat, certified halal and sourced from local farms — plus Middle Eastern grocery. Open seven days.

International Halal Market ROXBURY CROSSING
1433 Tremont St, Roxbury Crossing

Two minutes from Hebron Market and close to ISBCC. This stretch of Tremont is the halal grocery hub.

Garden Halal Meat HAYMARKET
96 Blackstone St, Boston

Butcher and fishmonger. Combine it with the Haymarket produce market.

Foodland Halal Market & Cafe MALDEN
11 Highland Ave, Malden

Flagged in the source list as the best of the group — the largest selection. Worth a car trip for bulk.

Sayar Market REVERE
3 Everett St #3E, Revere

Halal meat plus Burmese and South Asian groceries.

Waltham India Market WALTHAM
315 Moody St, Waltham

South Asian groceries — two doors from Waltham Islamic Society.

Buying for Eid?

Most of these butchers will do whole lamb or goat with about a week's notice.

Start a group run

A monthly SMBC run to Malden or Revere, split between a few students with a car, beats Cambridge retail.

Always ask

"Halal" on a US storefront is not a regulated claim. Ask about zabiha if that is your standard.

How we tier the restaurants

The source list mixes fully halal restaurants with places where only one item is halal. Publishing them as one undifferentiated list would be misleading, and could lead someone to eat something they would not choose to. So every restaurant below carries a tier.
1 Fully halal — the whole menu 2 Partially halal — specific items only 3 Safe to eat — no halal meat
